# PLAN100-10 — Execute the agreed requirements

> "The signed requirements package is executed by the planning tool, so the harness continues past the published handover site into the delivery build rather than ending there."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-080` · locus `S-02 where the harness ends`

## What this is for

The harness currently stops at a published handover site: the requirements are agreed, the package is delivered, and then the process ends and building starts somewhere else, by hand. This requirement carries the agreed package straight on into the build, so there is no manual step between what the client approved and what the machine starts working on. Practically it means the whole pipeline becomes one chain rather than two halves with a gap in the middle. Everything else in the delivery half of this milestone describes what the build then does; this is the only requirement that says what it is handed.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "There are capabilities we talked about that are missing. Preparing for the review meeting. Taking the feedback from that review meeting and using it to update all the requirements. Using GSD to then execute the requirements. An overnight mode that cleans it up. And in the morning they can pick up and see what it did."
> — the practitioner, `workshop-docs/practitioner-notes-2026-09-19.md` § *Capture — where the harness ends*

Three labelled one-step corrections stand against that passage at the source: the dictation's hesitations and its repeated "and" were removed; the opening was turned from a statement about the reader into a statement of what is missing; and "clean it up with an overnight mode" was read as an overnight mode that performs the clean-up. **The five capabilities themselves are unchanged and in his order.**

**The reasoning on the register.** **The row closes a seam no other row named.** The tracker requirement carries the planning tool's output into the workbook; this one carries the same package on into build. The run's own behaviour is held by the four night requirements — **what none of those states is what the run is handed.** It creates the sixth-stage directory and changes the two documents that both declare five stages.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** The row was minted against the practitioner's correction of the premise the register rested on: **the harness does not stop at the published handover site.** Two of his five capabilities — the overnight run and the morning pick-up — were deliberately given no row of their own, because writing one would have manufactured a gap the sheet does not have.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Harness* — the pod's own tooling and process for running delivery engagements. *GSD* — the planning tool. *Daily sprint* — the one-day cycle this stage sits inside.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **The name of the sixth-stage directory is not settled.** A working name was adopted so the stage is one kit and not two, but it is not the practitioner's decision. It is decided in this phase because this is what creates the directory, and every later phase writes into it.
- **Two consequences of that working name are already baked in** and are his to accept or change: the round files do not sort into the order the day runs them, and a reporting round sits inside the delivery kit because no reporting kit is proposed.
- **A stale claim travels with this work.** Two documents state that the pipeline has five stages, in five named places. Those statements become false the moment the sixth exists.
