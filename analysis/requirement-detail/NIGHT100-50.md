# NIGHT100-50 — Bound the automation

> "The automation may move and transform data, write and change code, run tests, and prepare and propose work. A human approves, decides and releases."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-130` · locus `S-01 18:01`

## What this is for

Once work runs unattended, the first question anyone asks — a client, a reviewer, a new team member — is what the machine is allowed to do without asking. If that answer lives in several people's judgment it will differ between them. This requirement puts it in one place, in both halves: what the automation performs, and what stays with a person. Its value is mostly to the business rather than to the build, because it is the sentence that makes an unattended run explainable to someone who was not there. It is a statement of policy, not a mechanism.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "Kind of where I'm at is maybe just an old developer in me, which is having a hard time letting it go, but there's something like having the architecture components in your head and then letting the AI roam within those boxes, but you're still building the boxes."
> — a delivery-team participant, `S-01 18:01`

> "AI has your wrenches and hammers now, but you're still the engineer responsible to make sure the building doesn't fall down."
> — the practitioner, `S-01 18:45`

**Read this pair as the origin of the boundary, not as its wording.** The register's own statement is a restatement of what the passage argues, not a transcription of a sentence anyone said. **A planner who expects to find that list in the transcript will not find it.** The passage is where the division comes from: the human builds the boxes and carries the responsibility; the automation works inside them.

**The reasoning on the register.** The automation boundary, permitted half and reserved half, in one row. **It was written as two rows** — the permission and its guardrail stated separately — **and the row carrying the reserved half was struck on review.** A permission left by itself would tell the business what the automation may do and nothing about what it may not, so both clauses were folded here.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** **Both halves must survive into the built file.** A plan that ships only the permitted list reproduces exactly the defect the fold was made to repair.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Cognitive load* — the reviewer's burden, treated as the binding constraint on delivery speed. *Harness*.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **The two halves are one thing.** Splitting them again, or shipping the permitted list alone, undoes the review finding that produced this row.
- The boundary is a written policy. Nothing in this requirement enforces it mechanically, so a later round should not assume it is a check.
- The reserved verbs — approve, decide, release — need a named human at run time, and this row does not say who.
