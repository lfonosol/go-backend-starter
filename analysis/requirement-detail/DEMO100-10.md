# DEMO100-10 — Generate the session pack

> "The daily session's materials are generated from the planning data as one pack, built by an interactive round in which the practitioner selects what the pack contains, rather than written by hand."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-200` · locus `S-02 the daily session pack` and `S-01 37:58`

## What this is for

The client-facing session happens every day, and preparing for it by hand every day is exactly the kind of recurring cost that stops a fast delivery rhythm from being sustainable. This requirement turns that preparation into one round: the planning data supplies the facts, the practitioner supplies the judgment about what is worth showing and asking, and the output is a single pack rather than three documents assembled separately. Its beneficiaries are the client, who gets a consistent session, and the person who would otherwise spend part of every day writing it.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "The daily session covers three things: a demonstration of what was built the night before, a review of what is to be built the next day so we have feedback, and a handover document so the business users can actually do UAT on what was built. The pack is generated interactively — by a prompt or a command that runs it — rather than by a batch script."
> — the practitioner, `workshop-docs/practitioner-notes-2026-09-19.md` § *Capture — the daily session pack*

> "One is almost, it's like the agenda setter for our meeting, our daily Sprint meeting."
> — the practitioner, `S-01 37:58`

**The reasoning on the register.** **The row replaces the agenda-only row the sheet carried: the pack is the deliverable and the agenda alone was never it.** The pack has three parts and each is a rule of its own.

**Judgment calls that bear on it, and this one is a decision rather than a detail.** **The build mechanism is a prompt and not a batch script.** A batch generator would read the planning data and emit a pack; an interactive round reads the same data and then asks which features are worth demonstrating, what to propose next, and what a tester should try — **because none of those three is derivable from the planning data alone.** That places the pack's builder in a prompt kit rather than beside the report generator. A second call is recorded against the three part-rows: they were left silent on interactivity because they are contents of one artifact and this row carries that artifact's mechanism — **repeating it on each part would state the same mechanism four times for one round.**

**RAID rows linked to it.** None directly. The dependency on the client's testers attaches to the UAT part.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Daily sprint* — a one-day cycle, framed in the session as a 90-minute client-facing meeting combining demo, planning and a walkthrough. *Harness*.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **One pack and one round is an untested choice.** The alternative was three artifacts with three rounds. If the UAT handover turns out to need a different audience, cadence or distribution, it separates out and this requirement shrinks.
- The three parts are separate requirements but share this row's mechanism. A plan that builds them independently will build three rounds.
- The pack's subject is the night that just finished, so it depends on the dispatch requirement and reads a run the practitioner has already closed.
