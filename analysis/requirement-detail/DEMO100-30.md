# DEMO100-30 — Propose what comes next

> "The pack carries what is proposed next, and the session's agreement becomes the next run's specifications."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-220` · locus `S-02 the daily session pack` and `S-01 30:30`

## What this is for

This is the part that closes the delivery loop. The session does not only report on the night that finished; it decides the night that follows, and that decision is written straight into what the next run is handed. The effect is that a client's steer in a morning meeting is being built the same evening, with no re-specification step in between — which is the whole reason the cycle can be a day long. It is also the point where a client has real influence, once a day, on what happens next.

## The evidence

**What the source says.** The same passage quoted on [DEMO100-20](DEMO100-20.md) carries this half — *"discussion of what we plan on doing"* — and the practitioner's notes state it as one of the pack's three parts.

**The reasoning on the register.** **This is the half that closes the delivery loop**: the agreement reached in the session is the input the delivery stage hands the next unattended run.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** **It is not the post-signature return path**, and confusing the two is the recorded hazard. That other path re-enters the registers; this one enters the build. **Confusing them would put a client's post-signature correction into a nightly build queue, and a build decision into a signed register.** As with the pack's other parts, this row is silent on the build mechanism because the pack's own row carries it once.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Daily sprint*. *Change register* — the artifact this path must not write into.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **Keep it apart from the requirements return path.** They look alike and go to different places; the register is the business's approval view and a nightly build decision does not belong in it.
- What is agreed in a meeting and what is written as a specification are not automatically the same thing. Nothing here says who writes the second from the first.
- The handoff it writes is read by the dispatch round, so its format is a contract between two phases.
