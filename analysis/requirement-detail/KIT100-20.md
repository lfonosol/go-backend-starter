# KIT100-20 — Drop the signature record

> "The documentation kit records no sheet signature: no round writes a digest-and-token row for a sheet, and no round gates on one. The repository's own history is what a later reader consults for what a sheet said and when, and the practitioner's acceptance of a sheet is recorded by the round that asked for it."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-070` · locus `S-02 the signature apparatus`

## What this is for

Every sheet the documentation process produces currently carries a signature record, and several rounds refuse to proceed without one. That apparatus costs work on every engagement and answers a question the repository's own history already answers better. Removing it takes a maintained duplicate out of the pipeline and shortens every documentation round that touched it. What it must not remove is the boundary between a draft and something the practitioner has accepted — that stays, carried by the round's own note and the commit that recorded the act. This is the only requirement in the milestone that makes the harness smaller.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "I don't think we need a signature requirement at all. I think it's a state you don't need to track. So that is why I am confused — what is the purpose of tracking that? Drop it, period. Not just from this engagement: we are going to track that I don't understand the value of even tracking this for any other engagement. Then I guess we need to add something to the backlog, and we will address it in the hardening stage, to get this out of the harness."
> — the practitioner, `workshop-docs/practitioner-notes-2026-09-19.md` § *Capture — the signature apparatus*

Two labelled one-step corrections stand at the source: three turns of one exchange are joined into one passage in his order, and the dictation's hesitations were removed. The sentences are otherwise his, **and the instruction to schedule it for hardening is inside the quotation.**

**The reasoning on the register — three grounds, in his own order of argument.**

1. **Git already does the job, and better.** Every change to every sheet carries an author, a timestamp and a full history. The signature record is a hand-maintained and weaker copy of that.
2. **The one thing git does not carry is approval as distinct from authorship.** Agents write the commits here, so the history shows that a change was made and not that a human accepted it. That distinction is real. It does not need a digest file; it needs the practitioner to say yes somewhere recorded, in his own words.
3. **The client-dispute ground does not survive contact, and it is the ground that settles it.** A digest inside our own repository, written by our own process, signed with a token our own agent typed, **is self-attested and is not evidence against a counterparty.** A real scope dispute is settled by the email the client answered, the meeting record and the document sent to them.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** The removal was scheduled for the hardening stage on the practitioner's own instruction, given inside the quotation above. That instruction, and not any dependency, is why this sits last.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None directly. One correction is owed: the risk about registers going stale still states that post-signature feedback re-enters the registers, which is true only while the kit signs. **This requirement is what makes that statement stale, and therefore what corrects it.**

**Glossary terms in play.** *Change register*. *Harness*.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **What must not go with it is the handover boundary.** A sheet the practitioner has accepted stops being a draft, and a later change goes through the apply-changes round. The replacement mechanism has an argument behind it but **has never been exercised** — test it by asking a real repository which sheets were accepted and when, not by checking that a line exists.
- **The per-finding signed column is a different mechanism and this row does not touch it.** It gates the apply-changes round; removing it would remove that gate. **It reads like part of the same apparatus and is not.** A removal sweep that takes it by association breaks something nothing else records.
- Whether that column should exist at all is a separate question the practitioner carried away. If the answer is that it also goes, that is new scope with its own row, and the gate needs a named replacement before anything is removed.
- **This is a wide, shallow change.** It reaches both prompt kits, the contracts, the pipeline and three of the board's test files.
