# SITE100-10 — Generate the tracker

> "The scope tracker is generated from the planning tool's own phases and plans rather than assembled by hand."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-020` · locus `S-01 01:16:04`

## What this is for

The scope tracker is the artifact both sides of this engagement named as the one with the most leverage — it is what the client reads to see what is in scope and what state it is in. Today somebody types it, which means it is only as current as the last person who remembered to update it, and it can quietly disagree with the plan it is supposed to describe. Making it a product of the planning data removes that drift by construction: a refresh reproduces it, and any row can be followed back to the plan that produced it. The people it changes things for are the client reading the tracker and the person who currently maintains it by hand.

The transcript renders MoSCoW as "Moscow" throughout. That is a speech-to-text mis-capture of the acronym, canonicalised in the glossary, and not a different term.

**The reasoning on the register.** Stated as the practitioner's intent for the next round, replacing the current practice of writing the tracker directly.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** **The generator carries no identifier of its own.** The prototype register that would have minted one was dropped from this engagement, and **no identifier was invented in its place** rather than leaving a hole that looks like an omission.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Scope tracker* — the spreadsheet carrying the scope statements and their MoSCoW ratings, which the handover site reads. *GSD* — the planning tool. *MoSCoW* — Must, Should, Could, Won't.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **One question is open and it is the practitioner's to answer: does an interactive round choose what goes into the tracker, or is the tracker a whole carry of the planning tool's phases and plans?** It was deliberately left silent, and the round that left it silent called it the closest call on the register. A selection over the tracker breaks the traceability the tracker exists for, so the answer changes what "generated" means here.
- The planning data may not carry everything a tracker row needs. A rating is the obvious candidate, because the session's own sequence is generate first, then rate. Any column with no source has to be named as an exception rather than discovered as a gap.
