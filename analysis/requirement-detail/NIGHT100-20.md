# NIGHT100-20 — Enter overnight mode

> "Entering overnight mode is performed by an interactive round of its own, in which the practitioner selects what the night's run takes and the round then dispatches it."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-100` · locus `S-02 the mode transitions`

## What this is for

Handing a night's work to a machine that nobody will watch is a decision, and a decision needs a moment. Without one, the transition happens by default and whatever happens to be queued is what runs. This requirement makes that moment explicit: a round that asks the practitioner what the night takes, shows him the selection, and only then dispatches it. What it buys is control at the one point where control is cheapest — before eight unattended hours rather than after them. For the person running the day it is the difference between choosing and discovering.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "I also expect a script or a prompt for entering overnight mode, for leaving it and entering morning mode, and for preparing the status report. I would expect those all to be interactive prompts. When we get to implementation details we can work out exactly what, but at the very least I want a chance to pick and choose what goes into each one."
> — the practitioner, `workshop-docs/practitioner-notes-2026-09-19.md` § *Capture — the mode transitions, and the weekly status report*

**The reasoning on the register.** The dispatch rhythm says when it happens, and three other rules say how the run behaves. **None of them states what performs the transition into the mode, and the harness has no file that does** — the dispatch specification is the run's own specification, not a round anyone runs at day's end.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** The test applied was **whether the new row adds an act or restates one.** Nothing on the sheet performed the switch into the mode, so this adds an act. **And the row deliberately does not state what its round asks, in what order, or in what form** — the practitioner set that aside: *"when we get to implementation details we can work out exactly what."* A plan that specifies the questions over-reaches the capture; a plan that reads the silence as an oversight misreads it. **His own test is the one to build against:** *"at the very least I'd want a chance to pick and choose what was picked for those."* **A round that reads the planning data and emits an artifact does not satisfy it, however good the artifact. A round that proposes a selection and lets him change it does.**

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Overnight mode* — one of four named modes of the practitioner's run loop.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **The questions the round asks are deliberately unspecified.** Do not invent them, and do not read the silence as an omission.
- The round dispatches as well as selects, so it sits on top of the dispatch requirement rather than beside it.
- Its mirror image, the round that closes the run in the morning, is a separate requirement in a different phase. The two should be designed as a pair even though they land apart.
