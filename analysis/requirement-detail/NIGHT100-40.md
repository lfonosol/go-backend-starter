# NIGHT100-40 — Stop on a broken gate

> "An unattended run that breaks a gate stops and waits, rather than working around the gate to keep going."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-120` · locus `S-01 25:45`

## What this is for

An agent that meets a rule it cannot satisfy has two options: stop, or find a way past. The second is far more dangerous overnight, because the workaround is invisible until someone finds it days later and cannot tell what else it touched. This requirement makes stopping the only option. The practical effect is on the morning: the team arrives to one named failure at a known point, which is a thing you can fix in an hour, instead of a completed run that quietly went around a check. It is the cheapest guardrail in the milestone and the one that protects every other gate the team owns.

## The evidence

**What the source says.**

> "If you break our gates, then you just stop. We come in the morning, okay, try to do this and fail. Then we solve that one part of the process."
> — the delivery lead, `S-01 25:45`–`25:51`

**The reasoning on the register.** A guardrail on the dispatch requirement, **stated as its own rule rather than folded into it.** The morning then begins with a named failure rather than with a silent workaround to discover.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** The round that wrote this kept it separate on a standing instruction: a guardrail is never folded into the rule it guards, so neither can be read without the other being visible.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Overnight mode*. *Harness*.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **Stopping is only useful if the stop is legible.** A run that halts without naming the gate, the point and the reason costs the morning as much as a workaround would.
- A stopped run leaves the rest of the night unused. That is the accepted cost of the rule, not a defect to engineer around.
- What counts as a gate has to be enumerable by the run itself, or the rule cannot be applied.
