# MORNING100-20 — Leave the run for morning mode

> "Leaving the overnight run and entering morning mode is performed by an interactive round of its own, in which the run is closed and the practitioner selects what the morning takes from it."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-150` · locus `S-02 the mode transitions`

## What this is for

A night's run has to be closed before its output can be reviewed, and today nothing performs that closing. Without it the run simply trails off and the morning picks over whatever state it was left in. This requirement puts a deliberate end to the night: the run is closed, and the practitioner chooses what the morning actually takes forward from it rather than inheriting everything. It is the point where a human re-enters the loop, and the selection made here is what keeps the review to a size one person can read.

## The evidence

**What the source says.** The same passage as [NIGHT100-20](NIGHT100-20.md), under the same heading — the practitioner expects a round for entering overnight mode, a round for leaving it and entering morning mode, and a round for the status report. He states his test for all three: *"at the very least I want a chance to pick and choose what goes into each one."*

**The reasoning on the register.** The morning review says the day opens with a review, and the decision log is the record that review reads. **Neither closes the run, and the harness has no file that does.**

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** The row **sits with the morning work rather than with the night work because the mode it enters is the morning one.** The same analysis recorded why the two logging rules were left silent on interactivity: **both are written by a process as it runs, one of them unattended overnight, so there is no moment at which a human picks their contents.** This round is where the selection over the night's record actually happens. As with its mirror, what the round asks and in what order is deliberately not stated.

**RAID rows linked to it.** None. No row on the RAID sheet links this requirement.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Overnight mode* — one of four named modes of the practitioner's run loop. *Morning report*.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **The questions this round asks are deliberately unspecified**, on the same instruction that governs its mirror at the start of the night.
- It and the entering round are a pair. Designing one without the other produces two rounds that disagree about what a run's boundaries are.
- The selection made here decides what the review sees. A round that selects badly hides work rather than filtering it.
