# KIT100-10 — Return the review's feedback

> "Feedback from a review meeting held after the workbook is signed re-enters the documentation stage as a source in its own right, and the registers and the tracker are corrected against it without renumbering."

MoSCoW and Status live in the shared Scope Tracker sheet, and the requirement's phase in `.planning/ROADMAP.md` — **look them up there; this card does not restate them.** The quotes below are the workshop-corpus record.

**Sources:** change record `CR-060` · locus `S-02 where the harness ends`

## What this is for

Once a workbook is signed, the pipeline treats the requirements as settled — and then the client sits in a review meeting and says something that changes them. At present that sentence has nowhere to go: it is not a finding the kit's own checks raised, so nothing in the process will accept it, and it ends up in somebody's notes. This requirement gives that feedback a door, so a correction reaches the registers and the tracker instead of being lost, and it does so without moving any identifier a client has already approved. It matters most for the client, whose second thoughts currently cost them a conversation and change nothing.

## The evidence

**What the source says.** Two of the practitioner's five named capabilities belong here:

> "There are capabilities we talked about that are missing. Preparing for the review meeting. Taking the feedback from that review meeting and using it to update all the requirements. Using GSD to then execute the requirements. An overnight mode that cleans it up. And in the morning they can pick up and see what it did."
> — the practitioner, `workshop-docs/practitioner-notes-2026-09-19.md` § *Capture — where the harness ends*

The source's own consequence note is the sharpest statement of the problem:

> "The return path is the part that does not exist in any form. The apply-changes round accepts only findings the kit's own validation rounds raised and signed: its inputs name two findings logs and no source, and its applicability conditions refuse everything else by name. Feedback from a meeting held after the workbook is signed therefore has no way in... Building the loop is design work on the pipeline's shape, not a row to add to a sheet."

**The reasoning on the register.** **The daily session is not this.** That session's feedback becomes delivery specifications inside the build loop and never reaches a register. The round this requirement needs is undesigned.

**Judgment calls that bear on it.** **This row's digest half is gone, and that is a deliberate shrinkage rather than an oversight.** A RAID issue once stood beside it asking whether a corrected sheet is signed again or returned for signing. **That row was struck because its premise was false**, not because its scope was gone: the documentation kit's own handoff already says in plain words that a sheet corrected after signing is signed again. It is **retired in place, its number never reused**, leaving a declared hole in the Issue block. A planner meeting a gap in the RAID numbering should read it as declared, not as a loss.

**RAID rows linked to it.**

| ID | Type | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| `R-020` | Risk | The registers go stale after the initial review. The change register was assessed as useful at the first client review and under-used afterwards, and the RAID register as not chased to the end |

The RAID sheet cites this row by its change record, `CR-060`, because that sheet has not moved.

**Glossary terms in play.** *Change register* — the log of changes and rules agreed with the client; its purpose is the test of whether any row belongs in it at all. *RAID* — risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies.

## Open items and watch-outs

- **This is design work before it is implementation.** No part of the return path exists, so there is nothing to extend.
- **A return path nobody walks is the failure mode to design against.** The registers were assessed as useful at the first review and under-used afterwards. That makes the no-renumbering clause a usability requirement as much as a correctness one: an identifier that moves is a reason not to come back.
- The signature apparatus this row's own reasoning refers to is being removed by [KIT100-20](KIT100-20.md). Whichever lands second has to carry the correction.
